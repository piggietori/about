Case Study 01 Security vs User Experience

Turning a security blocker into a self-service experience.

Results at a Glance
↓

Help Desk ticket volume through self-service resolution

↑

Security compliance rates across the device fleet

★

Enterprise-grade UX replacing a dead-end error page

The Problem

When a device failed a security health check, users hit a wall. They were redirected to a generic error page — told their device or authentication wasn't compliant, with no guidance on what to do next. The result: a flood of Help Desk tickets, frustrated engineers blocked from doing their jobs, and a security posture that relied entirely on manual intervention.

My Approach

I started with the users — not the engineers who built the system, but the people living inside it every day. Through user research and Help Desk data synthesis, I mapped four distinct personas:

🛠 Help Desk Admin

Needed visibility into device status to triage and resolve issues efficiently

✅ Proactive User

Wanted to stay ahead of compliance issues before they caused disruption

⚡ Reactive User

Needed to get unblocked fast after hitting a security issue mid-workflow

💻 High Device Count User

Required a structured exemption path to do their job without constant friction

From there, I designed three core flows: self-remediation, short-term exemption, and long-term exemption — each tailored to how that user actually experiences the problem.

The Solution

A guided remediation and exemption experience that gave users agency. Instead of a dead-end error page, users now land in a flow that diagnoses the issue, walks them through resolution steps, and — when remediation isn't immediately possible — provides a structured path to request time-bound or role-based exemptions.

The design balanced two competing pressures: security rigor (the company's need) and user autonomy (the engineer's need). Neither could be compromised.

Case Study 02 AI Playbook

Building an AI-native team from the ground up.

Results at a Glance

20

The Problem

Most product teams in 2022 were AI-curious at best. There was no shared language, no consistent skill baseline, and no structured path for PMs and designers to meaningfully adopt AI in their daily work. The risk: a team that falls behind as AI reshapes how products are built — and how fast.

My Approach

I didn't buy a training program. I built one from scratch, designed specifically for the realities of our team and the pace of change in AI. The playbook was built around four pillars: fundamentals, system design, product sense, and evaluation frameworks — each one chosen because it maps directly to how PMs do their jobs, not how engineers do theirs.

What I Built

🧠 AI Essentials Curriculum

Custom upskill program covering fundamentals through evaluation frameworks, built for PMs and designers specifically

🤖 Agent-Driven Workflows

Re-engineered daily team workflows using autonomous agents, replacing manual processes across business reviews, performance management, and product health analysis

🏘 AI Community

Built internal knowledge-sharing community to sustain continuous learning and peer growth beyond formal training

Shipped AI solutions for MBRs, performance management, and product health analysis used across 7 engineering teams
